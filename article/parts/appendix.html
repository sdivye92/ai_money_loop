This appendix holds the machinery behind the story. It sets out the model in formal terms, every input with its source and the assumptions with how much they move the results. It also lists what could not be sourced and gives the full source list. None of it is needed to follow Parts 1 to 8. All of it is here so that every number in them can be checked. To run the model yourself, open the companion page Inside the Model.

The model in formal terms

Each firm i has an annual AI revenue at stake, Ai. For the big clouds and neoclouds it is contracted backlog spread evenly over the contract life (five years unless disclosed; Amazon discloses 6.1[20]). Suppliers use the AI share of trailing revenue times trailing revenue while labs use revenue. The map gives, for every seller i, the share sji of Ai paid by buyer j. Annual flows are Fji = sji Ai.

A shock is a vector c0: the share of each origin's outflows that it cuts. In each round t the loss to firm i is

Revenue loss in round t\[ L_i(t)=\sum_j c_j(t)F_{ji} \]

and each firm's own cut for the next round is

Next-round spending cut\[ c_i(t+1)=\max\left(c_i^0,\min\left(1,\frac{\epsilon L_i(t)}{B_i}\right)\right) \]

where Bi is Ai for firms whose outflows are AI-specific and total revenue for firms whose outflows are firm-wide capex (TSMC, the memory makers, Intel). \(\epsilon=1\) means a firm cuts spending in the same proportion as it loses AI revenue. A firm that crosses its break line sets \(c_i=1\) from then on. The rounds repeat until no cut changes by more than \(10^{-7}\).

Break lines. A self-funded firm (positive EBITDA, debt under 5 times EBITDA) breaks when \(\left(\frac{\mathrm{debt}}{\mathrm{EBITDA}}\right)_{0}\,\times\,\frac{\mathrm{EBITDA}}{\mathrm{EBITDA}-g_iL_i}>5\). Here gi is gross margin and the threshold is 7.5 for the two REITs. Breaking means it freezes AI capex. A financing-dependent firm (every neocloud and landlord except the REITs, plus any firm with debt at 5 times EBITDA or more) breaks when Li reaches 20% of Ai. Then its lenders lose 60% of its debt.

Without break lines the update is linear and its fixed point is the Leontief solution \(\mathbf{c}=(\mathbf{I}-\epsilon\mathbf{W})^{-1}\mathbf{c}^{0}\), where \(W_{ij}=\frac{F_{ji}}{B_i}\). In this map W has no cycles: no payment goes back up the chain, so W is nilpotent (its spectral radius is 0) and \((\mathbf{I}-\epsilon\mathbf{W})^{-1}\) is a finite sum. A shock passes down at most once per layer. The circular part of the deals is financial (equity stakes, guarantees and loans) and is measured in the equity and lender channels instead. Only the break lines are non-linear.

Stress score. For each listed firm, five signals are ranked across the 53 listed firms: negative free cash flow over revenue, net debt over EBITDA (capped at 20), the largest buyer's share of AI revenue, market value over revenue and the one-year price fall. The score is the mean of the percentile ranks that exist for that firm.

Assumptions and how much they move the results

The map: every seller and its buyers

For each seller: its AI revenue at stake, how that figure was built and the share paid by each buyer. Letters mark the source of each share: S disclosed, E estimate from contracts or reports, G allocated in proportion to the buyer's capex (or, for the foundry and memory makers, to chip designers' cost of goods).

The financial panel

Every listed firm's inputs. USD billions; trailing twelve months unless stated. Korean firms converted at 1,446 won per dollar for flows (2026 average) and 1,336 for balances (30 October[112]); SoftBank at about 156 yen.

Extensions in Parts 6 and 7. Contagion: after an origin fails \(c_i=1\), every financing-dependent firm k also cuts \(c_k^0=\phi x_k\). Here \(x_k=\frac{\text{capex}-\text{operating cash flow}}{\text{capex}}\) is the share of its spending paid with outside money (80% for private firms without cash-flow data, an assumption) and \(\phi\) is the share by which lenders and investors cut new money (30% or 60%). Reflexive test: \(c_k^0=0.35x_k\) for neoclouds and landlords and \(c^0=\frac{1}{3}(0.648)\) for OpenAI, whose outside-funded share of spending in the map is 64.8%. Policy test: builders cut only purchases from equipment sellers by d (10%, 20% or 30%); purchases of rented capacity from clouds, neoclouds and landlords are exempt from the outside cut but not from knock-on cuts. Event tests: profit at stake = \(\frac{g_iL_i}{\mathrm{EBITDA}_i}\), compared with the one-day price fall. Market value at risk: each group's value times its observed fall from early October to early November (neoclouds 35%, chip index 15.4%, platforms 10%). Lender recovery: forced-sale value 0.6 times 100 times 0.8age over the balance (65 for a bullet loan, 65 amortised straight-line over five years), capped at 100%.

Financing feedback (Part 3). Each funding tie f → r from the deal database gets a weight φfr = gr × (f's share of r's outside money by committed amount), where gr is the share of r's spending paid with outside money. A funder's stress pf is the share of its AI revenue it has lost. It is 1 if the funder has broken. SoftBank's stress is OpenAI's cut. Lenders' stress is their loss rate on loans to neoclouds and landlords. Each round, recipient r's funding cut is \(x_r=\min\left(1,\kappa\sum_f\phi_{fr}p_f\right)\). Its next cut is \(c_r=1-\left(1-\max\left(c_r^0,\min\left(1,\frac{\epsilon L_r}{B_r}\right)\right)\right)(1-x_r)\). With \(\kappa=0\) this is the payments-only model used in Parts 4 to 7. At \(\kappa=1\) the linearised system has a spectral radius of 0.54 and would turn unstable only at \(\kappa\approx4.0\). Four judgements shape the weights. AMD's warrants are left out. The reported $350B of Nvidia financing is replaced by the signed $105B guarantee. SoftBank's funding follows OpenAI's cut. A funding cut lowers spending without breaking a firm by itself. Every weight, the sensitivity to κ and the lab are on the companion page Inside the Model.

What could not be sourced

  • Customer-level revenue for the suppliers. Nvidia, AMD, TSMC and the memory makers do not name their customers. Their links are allocations, flagged G.
  • The share of each supplier's AI revenue bought by firms outside the map. Set at 30% (35% for the tool makers, 50% to 70% for Dell, HPE and the storage makers). An assumption.
  • Covenant terms for every neocloud. The 20% line is calibrated on CoreWeave with an assumed 1.5 times covenant.
  • Nvidia's 2026 data-centre share (the FY2025 share of 88% is used), AI shares for Marvell, SK hynix, Samsung, Sandisk, Western Digital, HPE, Cisco, Celestica, Vertiv, Eaton, GE Vernova, Constellation, Vistra, Equinix and Digital Realty. All flagged as estimates.
  • Fluidstack's customer split, Crusoe's contract split and debt, Lambda's backlog. Press reports only.
  • Microsoft's share of CoreWeave's backlog after 2025 (set at 30%); OpenAI's share of Microsoft's backlog (the dataset's $250B gives 36.9%; one estimate gives about 32%).
  • TSMC's trailing financials: the release gives the quarter, so revenue is the September quarter times four; debt and cash come from an older page.
  • OpenAI's losses and usage data; Anthropic's listing date and price.
  • A dated snapshot of market values: the companiesmarketcap.com page is undated and the statistics pages run from 26 October to 5 November.
  • Any history of neocloud defaults to calibrate against. None has occurred.
  • How much of the 5 November fall was due to expected policy changes. Profit-taking, retail selling, weak chip results and rate expectations moved prices the same day.
  • How long state permit pauses will last and how much capacity they cover. UBS (about 30% of 2028 additions at risk) and Goldman Sachs (temporary pauses) are the only estimates found[72]; the slowdown test uses 10% to 30% for a year.
  • Two reports could not be accessed: CNBC on Democrats' data-centre plans (5 November) and Politico on a Democratic "AI reckoning" (4 November). Their content is not used.
  • Usage data from the labs (tokens, active users over time), so a usage slowdown cannot be tested directly.
  • Amazon's quarterly free cash flow and operating costs for Q3 2026 in a primary source.
